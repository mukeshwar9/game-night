GAME NIGHT · DESIGN BOARD · ONE OF EIGHT MINIGAME STUDIES

LAZY SUSAN

A plate of dumplings turns in the middle of the table. Everyone holds one pair of chopsticks and one button. Tap when food passes your side, and you eat it. Tap at the wrong moment and you lose a point. Two to four players on one phone, or against a bot.

This is Game Night's own take on the shared-plate reflex minigame from JindoBlu's party app ("The Last Sashimi"). The mechanic was studied from gameplay video; the name, art, food, sounds and code here are new. Design source for this page: Game Night's own theme tokens and pixel font, because the board previews a Game Night screen.

WHAT IS ON THE PLATE

Dumpling: 1 point. Most of the plate.
Steamed bun: 3 points. One per plate, so everyone watches it.
Chili: lose 2 points and your chopsticks are frozen for 0.9 seconds. Let it go by.
Last bite: double. The final piece on a plate turns gold and the plate speeds up.

CONTROLS

Touch: tap your side of the screen. The coloured gate on the plate is your reach, and it lights up when something is inside it.

Keyboard (click the plate first): two players SPACE and ENTER. Three players SPACE P Q. Four players Z M P Q.

WHY IT IS FUN

  • One shared plate. Every piece you take is one your friend does not get, so each grab is felt by everyone.
  • The rules explain themselves. Food turns, chopsticks jab. Nobody needs a tutorial.
  • The 3-point piece. Everybody tracks the same piece at once, which creates the shouting.
  • The miss penalty. It stops button mashing and gives a nervous player a real way to lose.

WHERE IT FALLS SHORT

  • Only one decision. Every piece is good, so the only question is when. It gets flat after a few rounds. (My judgement.)
  • Seat luck. With the plate turning one way, the player upstream gets first pick of each new piece. (Follows from the layout; assumed for the original.)
  • No comeback. A player who is 8 behind has nothing to hope for.
  • Unfair robots. Store reviews of the app say hard bots win by getting advantages rather than by playing well, and that touch targets are too small (reviews dated 20 Dec 2024 and 30 Aug 2026).

THE GAME NIGHT VERSION

Same core, with the four weak points above answered. Every number is a starting value to be tuned in playtests.

A ROUND

  • 3-2-1, then the plate turns with 5 to 7 pieces on it.
  • Your gate is the coloured arc on your side. Tap while a piece is inside it to eat it.
  • Tap on nothing: lose 1 point and wait 0.45 seconds.
  • When the food is gone a new plate arrives, a little faster.
  • First to the target wins the match.

FAIR BY DESIGN

  • Each new plate turns the opposite way, so no seat is always upstream.
  • New pieces cannot be grabbed for their first 0.3 seconds, so nobody scores from a lucky spawn.
  • Scores never go below zero.
  • The bot presses the same button with human-like timing error. It never gets a head start.
  • Shapes differ, not only colours: crescent, round, long pepper.

FEEL

  • The tap resolves on contact; the animation follows. Nothing waits for a network reply.
  • A tap in the last 0.12 seconds of a wait is remembered and fires when the wait ends.
  • The plate has weight: it slows, stops and swings back when it reverses.
  • Each event has two signals: a sound and a visible change.
  • Reduced motion: no screen shake.

WHERE EVERYONE SITS

2 PLAYERS

Short ends. Each player owns half the screen.

3 PLAYERS

Gates 120 degrees apart: one at the bottom, two sharing the top half.

4 PLAYERS

Corners. Each player owns a quarter of the screen, so thumbs do not collide.

ONLINE

Same plate on every phone, turned so your own gate is nearest you.

FIVE TWISTS

The first three are in the prototype above and are the ones I recommend shipping in the first version. They are cheap and each one fixes a named weakness of the original.

SHIP FIRST1

THE TURN

Every new plate turns the other way, and you see it slow down and swing back.

WHY
Removes seat luck. Also resets everyone's rhythm, so a leader cannot coast on muscle memory.
RISK
Low. The reversal must be telegraphed (bell, visible slowdown, arrows on the hub) or it reads as a cheat.
COST
Small.
SHIP FIRST2

HOT CHILI

One piece on the plate is a chili. Grab it and you lose 2 points and freeze for 0.9 seconds.

WHY
Adds a second decision: what to leave. It punishes tapping on rhythm and makes a chili sitting next to the bun a real trap.
RISK
Low to medium. Too many chilies and the game becomes waiting. Start at one, two from the fourth plate.
COST
Small.
SHIP FIRST3

LAST BITE

The final piece on every plate turns gold, is worth double, and the plate speeds up by 45 percent.

WHY
Gives every plate a climax and gives the trailing player a swing: a golden bun is 6 points. This is the game's signature moment.
RISK
Medium. At high speed the gate may be too hard to hit. Watch the miss rate on the gold piece.
COST
Small.
LATER4

TWO RINGS

An inner ring turns the other way and carries the high-value pieces. A tap reaches the outer ring; press and hold to reach further in.

WHY
A risk choice on every pass: the safe dumpling now, or commit longer for the bun.
RISK
Medium. Breaks the one-tap simplicity; a hold on a shared phone is harder to read.
COST
Medium.
LATER5

BANQUET (CO-OP)

Everyone is on one team. A ticket shows whose turn it is to eat what; the kitchen sends plates faster until three mistakes end the run.

WHY
Turns the stealing game into a calling-out game. Fits Game Night's co-op flag and gives couples a non-competitive mode.
RISK
Medium. Needs its own tuning and a best-run score.
COST
Medium.
GAME NIGHT HOOK+

WINNER FEEDS THE NIGHT

Not a rule change: the match reports placements to the night scoreboard like every other game, and seats show each player's avatar on their chopstick band.

WHY
It is a 60-second palate cleanser between longer games, which is exactly what a game night needs.
RISK
None beyond the build.
COST
Comes with the room shell.

HOW ONLINE CAN WORK WITHOUT A REFEREE

The plate's angle is a formula of a shared start time, so phones never send positions to each other. Eating a piece is a single claim that only the first writer can make. Two gates are never over the same piece at once, and the gap between gates is at least 0.3 seconds of travel at top speed, which is longer than normal network delay. If you tap a piece someone upstream took a moment ago, you see TAKEN and lose nothing.
