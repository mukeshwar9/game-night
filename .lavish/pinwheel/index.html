QUIVER

A wooden wheel spins in the middle of the phone. Everyone gets the same small quiver of arrows and one button, and everyone shoots at the same time. Hit a star for a point; hit an arrow already in the wheel and yours clinks off and costs you one.

This is Game Night's own take on the spinning-target throwing game in the source app. Changed after your note: it is not turn-based. Each player has a limited set of arrows per wheel and all shoot simultaneously. The working name is now QUIVER because the game is about spending a limited quiver (PINWHEEL is still on the list below).

WHAT THE SOURCE GAME DOES

Studied from the store listing, the game's own rule card and four gameplay videos. Nothing was installed or copied; the drawing below is ours.

Players take turns. One tap throws a blade straight at the spinning target from your own end. Prizes on the rim score; blades stay stuck and crowd the rim.

HOW QUIVER PLAYS

Everyone shoots whenever they like until their quiver is empty. Four things can happen when your arrow reaches the wheel.

STAR · +1

Land on a star. The star is yours and your arrow stays in its place.

CLINK · −1

Land on any arrow, yours or theirs. Your arrow bounces off and is gone, you lose a point (never below zero), and your button locks for 0.8 seconds.

BARE RIM · 0

Your arrow sticks and becomes a wall for everybody, you included. Good for guarding a star.

LAST STAR · NEXT WHEEL

When the stars are gone, every quiver is empty or 15 seconds are up, arrows fly off and a new wheel deals in with full quivers. It spins faster, then it wobbles, then it reverses.

WHERE EVERYONE SITS ON ONE PHONE

Each pad is a 76-pixel thumb target that also shows that player's score, turned to face them. Every lane points at the hub, so every seat reaches every part of the rim equally: the wheel brings it round.

MATCH FLOW

  • No turns. After a 3·2·1, everybody shoots at once. A shot takes 0.4 seconds to reload, so mashing is possible but wastes arrows.
  • Limited arrows. A fresh quiver each wheel: 8 each with two players, 6 with three, 5 with four. There are fewer stars than arrows, and every miss leaves a wall on the rim.
  • Three wheels, 15 seconds each. A wheel ends when its stars are gone, all quivers are empty, or the 15 seconds run out, so nobody can hold up the table. Unused arrows are worth nothing.
  • Most stars after wheel 3 wins. A tie plays one sudden-death star with one arrow each.
  • Online: the same game in a room for 2–4, your own lane at the bottom of your screen. Each shot is saved as one number (the moment it was fired); the room database puts all shots in one order and every phone replays that order on the same seeded wheel.
  • Solo: you against a bot with the same quiver, whose only handicap is how well it times its shot.

LOOK AND MOTION NEW THIS ROUND

The play field is drawn as real objects under one light, and everything that happens is animated. Changed after your note: it now follows the selected theme. The shapes, lighting and motion stay the same; every colour is worked out from that theme's own colour tokens. Try the ten themes in the picker beside the prototype. All of it is painted in code, so the game needs no image files.

MATERIALS AND LIGHT

  • Wheel: a sawn log slice with growth rings, drying cracks, bark edge and paint soaked into the grain. The wood turns; the highlight and shade stay put, so it reads as lit from the top-left.
  • Arrows: steel broadhead, wooden shaft and feather fletching in the player's colour. Your remaining arrows are fanned out beside your button.
  • Prizes: bevelled metal stars on brass pegs; a domed bomb with a lit fuse; an enamel reverse token.
  • Depth: soft shadows under the wheel, each arrow and each prize, on a woven cloth in the theme's background colour.
  • Per theme: player colours are the theme's four player colours; stars use its action colour; the paint ring its danger colour. A one-colour theme (AMBER CRT) soaks the wood in that colour; 1-BIT MONO turns everything grey.
  • Pads: domed arcade buttons that sink when pressed.

IMPACTS

  • Stick: the arrow shivers and settles, wood chips and dust fly, the wheel recoils on a spring.
  • Clink: a 60 ms freeze, sparks, a shock ring, screen shake; the thrown arrow tumbles away and the struck arrow shivers.
  • Star: a burst of gold sparks and a ring; the score on the pad pops.
  • Bomb: flash, smoke, every arrow blown off under gravity.
  • In flight: a light streak, the arrow shrinking and its shadow closing in as it drops onto the wheel.

IDLE LIFE AND EASING

  • Stars bob and catch a glint in turn; the gold star throws slow rays; the fuse sputters.
  • Dust drifts through the light; the live lane's chalk dashes march toward the wheel; the active pad breathes.
  • New wheels and prizes pop in with overshoot; call-outs punch in and lift away; the wheel banner is a glass plate that slides in.
  • Phone-smooth: every textured object is painted once and reused; at most 220 particles. Measured in a desktop browser only so far.
  • Reduced-motion setting: no shake or freeze, a third of the particles.

PLAY IT

A working prototype of the core loop: no turns, limited arrows. On a phone, put it flat on the table and sit at opposite ends. With a mouse, click the pads. All the chosen twists are on. You can switch each off on the right to feel the difference; each change deals a fresh match.

THE TWISTS THAT ARE IN

Your call: all of them except Take Turns. Three are rules of the main game and are on in the prototype; the fourth is a co-op mode you can switch to.

CLOSE SHAVE IN · RULE

CHANGES
An arrow that lands in the narrow band right beside another arrow, without touching, scores +1.
WHY
With no star in reach you still have something brave to try. The best spot is the most dangerous one.
RISK
The band is not shown before the shot yet; it needs a clear flash so players learn it, and it must stay narrow or it out-scores stars.
COST
SMALL

RIM ITEMS IN · RULE

CHANGES
Each wheel also deals a gold star (+3), a bomb (blows every arrow off the rim) and a reverse token.
WHY
One moment per wheel worth fighting over; the bomb un-jams a crowded rim.
RISK
Gold can decide a match alone; one per wheel.
COST
SMALL

UNDERDOG SIGHT IN · RULE

CHANGES
The player who is strictly last gets a glowing marker on the rim showing where an arrow shot right now would land.
WHY
A comeback rule that helps the weakest player without taking anything from the leader.
RISK
Strong players may hang back to keep it. It disappears the moment you are no longer last.
COST
SMALL

CLEAR THE WHEEL IN · CO-OP MODE

CHANGES
One team, three shared hearts, same quivers, everyone shooting at once. Clear three wheels; a clink costs a heart.
WHY
Same wheel, opposite mood: you call out each other's timing instead of racing.
RISK
Needs more wheel patterns to stay interesting past a few clears.
COST
