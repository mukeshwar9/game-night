BAMBOOZLE

Bamboo poles fire across a walled garden from one side at a time. A pole stops when it hits a boulder, so the only safe ground is behind a rock, and there is never enough of it for everyone. Three hearts each; last one standing wins.

This is Game Night's own take on the "Spike Attacks" minigame in JindoBlu's 1 2 3 4 Player Games. The mechanic is studied; the name, art, sound and code here are new.

1 · How the source game playsRead from the app's own how-to screen and from gameplay videos. I did not install or open the app.

  1. A square yard of 4×4 tiles with three stones. Four spikes wait on each of the four walls, one per lane. [V1][V2][V3]
  2. One wall flashes red, then all its spikes shoot across and pull back. The in-app tip reads: "Hide behind the stones so you don't get hit by the spikes." [V1][V2]
  3. Each player steers a small character with a joystick in their own corner of the screen. The counter beside it starts at 3 and drops on each hit. [V3]
  4. Out at zero; the last one left wins. A medal screen ranks the players ("RED WINS!"). [V2][V3]
  5. Solo play is against bots with an EASY / MEDIUM / HARD slider. [V1][V2]

Not confirmed, treated as assumptions: whether the stones ever move inside a round (they stayed put in the footage I saw); whether the pace speeds up; whether more than one wall can fire at once; how four players are placed (I saw two corners used, and assume the other two corners for players three and four).

What players say: one store review lists "spike attacks" among the games it recommends for groups [R1]. The clips show the same two feelings again and again: a scramble for the same safe tile, and a hit that comes from misreading which side a stone protects.

2 · Game Night's versionThe same read-the-cover idea, with a telegraph you can learn, a round that is guaranteed to end, and cover that does not stay put.

Rules

GoalBe the last dodger with a heart left.
Yard4×4 tiles and 3 boulders for two players; 5×5 and 5 boulders for three or four.
HeartsThree each. A hit costs one and gives 1.3 s of blinking safety.
EscalationFrom volley 7 a second wall can fire with the first; from volley 15 two walls always fire.
FairnessA wall may fire only if the walk to cover from the worst tile in the yard fits inside the warning. When no wall fits, the kindest one fires. This depends on the boulders alone, so it is the same for every player online.
Round endOne dodger left wins the round. If the last two fall to the same volley it is a draw and nobody scores.
MatchOne phone and solo: first to two rounds. Online race rooms: the room's usual first to three.

Every number on this board is a starting value for playtests, not a measured one. The report lists each with the test that would move it.

Touch controls

  1. Thumb pad. Put a thumb down anywhere in your own strip and drag; the pad appears under it and follows a thumb that drifts.
  2. Nothing else in the base game. One input means a phone can be handed to anyone.
  3. With SHOVE on: a quick tap in your strip is a short dash that knocks rivals aside.
  4. When you are out, with HAUNT on: push the pad toward a wall to choose where the next volley comes from.

Why it should feel good

Each volley answers with four things at once: the poles overshoot and shudder, the yard shakes, dust and rock chips fly, and a low thud plays. A hit squashes the dodger, throws stars and plays a falling tone. Sound and shake land on contact, never after a network reply.

3 · Play itThe real loop, running in this page. Press a green button to play against the bot. Two people can also share a phone or a keyboard.

Play against the bot, right here

You are the one with the bouncing YOU tag. Mouse or finger: press anywhere on the game and drag the way you want to run. Keyboard: arrow keys or W A S D. With GRAB on: stand next to a rival and hold Space, the right mouse button, a second finger or the GRAB button; walk; let go to throw. What to look for

  1. Can you tell which tiles are safe before the hint tint is gone (volley 4 onward)?
  2. Does the scramble for one tile feel funny or unfair when a bot beats you to it?
  3. Is the round length right, or does it drag before two walls start firing?

3b · You are the dodgerAsked in review: can players use their own avatar, and can it be 3D? Yes to the first, now in the prototype. The second has three levels.

In the prototypeSmall

A · STANDEE

Your profile avatar's full-body picture, stood up in the yard. The game adds a darker side for thickness, a shadow cast on the sand, a hop when it runs, a lean, a squash when hit and the kit's own cheer when it wins. These four are real output of Game Night's avatar kit, so every look works, paid cosmetics included, with no new art.

Separate spikeMedium–large

B · BLOCK FIGURE

A true 3D figure built by turning each avatar pixel into a small cube. The catch: the kit only draws the front. A back and sides would be made up by rule (hair and outfit colours carried round), with a depth table for each part type. Most looks would come out well; some hats and hair would need hand fixes. It would let the figure turn to face where it runs.

Not advisedLarge

C · MODELLED 3D

Hand-made 3D parts for every hair, hat and outfit in the kit. It would look best and cost the most: every cosmetic would need a second, 3D version, for ever. Not worth it for one minigame.

My advice: ship A with the game. If you want B, cost it once as its own piece of work, because all eight new games could use the same figures.

4 · TwistsSix ways to make it more than a clone. Five are playable above; press a button to load one.

Ship firstSmall

CRUMBLE

Change
Cover has hit points. A boulder breaks on its third block and a new one lands on a marked tile.
Why
The source's fixed stones let you learn four answers and stop thinking. Moving cover makes every volley a fresh read.
Risk
A boulder landing on a dodger. Handled: the landing ring shows for a second and dodgers are nudged out, never hurt.
Cost
Small. It follows from the volley list, so every online client agrees without extra data.
Ship firstSmall

BAIT

Change
One coin lies on an open tile between volleys. Three coins buy back a heart, up to four.
Why
It gives the player who is behind a way back, and gives the leader a reason to leave cover. Camping stops being the best plan.
Risk
Fast players snowball. Starting rule: a coin can never push you above four hearts, and only one coin is ever out.
Cost
Small on one phone. Online the coin spot comes from the seed; whoever writes the pickup first keeps it.
Second passMedium

SHOVE

Change
A tap is a short dash on a 2.2 s recharge. Dash into a rival and they slide out of the tile they were hiding on.
Why
It turns dodging beside each other into playing against each other. This is the loudest laugh in the prototype.
Risk
Needs players who can touch. That works on one phone and in a two-player online duel, not in a seeded race of ghosts.
Cost
Medium. One phone is cheap; online means a host-run two-player duel on the existing peer link.
New · asked in reviewMedium

GRAB

Change
Stand beside a rival and hold GRAB. They are stuck to your hands: walk and they go where you push. Let go and they are thrown about a tile. You can hold for 1.4 s, then wait 3 s.
Why
You can drag someone out from behind a rock and throw them into the poles. Louder than SHOVE, and it asks for timing: let go too early and they walk back.
Counter
The held player wriggles free by pushing their own pad for half a second, and the grabber walks slower and is out of cover too. A hit on the grabber breaks the hold.
Risk
It needs a second control: a GRAB button beside each pad. Four players on one phone then have eight touch targets, which is tight. Needs contact, so one phone and the two-player duel only.
Cost
Medium. It is SHOVE's bigger sibling: I would ship one of the two, not both.
Second passSmall–medium

HAUNT

Change
When you are out you become a ghost on the wall. Push your pad toward a side and the next volley comes from there.
Why
In the source, a knocked-out player just watches. Here the first one out gets the most say over who wins.
Risk
Two ghosts ganging up on the leader. Starting rule: only the latest push counts, and a wall with no safe tile cannot be picked.
Cost
Small on one phone. Medium online: one new room key and its rule and rules test.
LaterMedium

HOLD THE LINE

Change
Co-op. One shared pool of hearts, a set number of volleys to outlast, and a downed friend gets up if you stand beside them for a volley.
Why
Game Night already has a co-op flag and co-op players. Sharing two safe tiles between four people is a different, kinder game.
Risk
Too easy with good players. Starting rule: boulders start already cracked and the wave count rises each win.
Cost
Medium. Not in the prototype.

Also considered and left out: one player firing the walls by hand while the rest dodge. It is the same idea as HAUNT with a worse wait for the others, so HAUNT covers it.

5 · Who sits whereOne phone, then online. The yard is the same in every case; only the pads move.

Online, the cheap way Recommended

Game Night's race rooms already give every player the same seeded content on the server clock and rank them at the end (Chop Chop, Mine Race). Bamboozle fits that shape: the seed decides the boulders and every volley; each phone runs only its own dodger; hearts and "out at" times are the stats the room ranks.

You see the others as ghosts, updated a few times a second. You cannot bump them. No new database rules, 2–8 players, and it works on weak connections because nothing fast crosses the network.

Online, with contact Only if SHOVE ships

The peer link the arena games use (Sumo, Tron) is one host and one guest. A two-player duel with shoving fits it as it is. Three or four players with shoving would need a new mesh or relay, which is a large piece of work on its own.

Closest existing games: Chop Chop shares "dodge the beam" but is a tap race with no movement; Sumo Arena shares the shoving but has no cover; Tron and Snake share last-one-standing. None has cover you read under time pressure, so this does not duplicate them.
