Bonk Buggies

A two-player buggy duel seen from the side. Drive, tilt and land on the other driver's helmet to score; touch your own helmet on anything and you hand them the point. The tide comes in so no round can stall.

Studied
“Crash It”, a two-player mini game in JindoBlu's 1 2 3 4 Player Games (the Play Store link in the request) and its sister app 2 Player Games: The Challenge.
Players
2. Online on two phones, two on one phone, or solo against a bot.
A match
First to 5 points on arenas about 18 m wide. Two bots at full throttle finish a round in 4 to 6 seconds on average; people take longer.
Changed 10 Oct
After the first review: arenas are 60 % wider with a camera that follows both buggies, top speed is lower and the tide starts later, so rounds run longer. A theme picker shows the game in eight themes.
Closest games here
Sumo Arena (shrinking ring), Air Hockey and Puck Rush (same room and one-phone pattern). None is a side-view driving duel.
Build
Large, at the low end: about 8 to 10 working days. No database or server changes beyond three room keys.
Recommended
Ship the duel with twists T1 Hop, T2 Spare lid and T3 Loser picks. Add T4 Wild rounds as a host switch. Keep T5 Melee for later.

Pick a theme

The arena takes its sky, rock and water from each theme's ordinary colour tokens, so it follows whatever theme the player has on. Tap one to switch the whole board and the prototype. Tokens are read from the app's stylesheet when this page is built.

Glass and Glass night use hand-picked scene colours. The other six are derived by one rule; some of those want tuning (1-bit mono's water, for one).

The Game Night version

Same heart, our own body. The setting is a harbour at low tide instead of a city skyline, the cars are open dune buggies with a helmeted driver and a pennant, and all of it is drawn in code from theme tokens. Nothing is taken from the source app.

Rules

  1. Two buggies start facing each other. After 3-2-1 the brakes release.
  2. Bonk: any part of your buggy touching the rival's helmet wins you the round.
  3. Own lid: your helmet touching the ground, a plank or a wall gives the rival the round.
  4. Splash: sinking below the water line, or leaving the arena, gives the rival the round.
  5. After 10 seconds the tide rises. Higher ground buys time, never safety.
  6. Both helmets hit in the same instant, or both buggies sink together: no point, the round replays.
  7. First to 5 points wins the match. Every round brings a new arena, and the buggies swap sides.

Controls

Two buttons per player, placed where thumbs rest. LEFT and RIGHT drive. In the air the same buttons tilt the buggy, the way the engine's kick would: RIGHT lifts the nose of a buggy facing right. Feedback fires on the press, not after the network answers.

Edge cases

  • Stuck on its side with no helmet contact and no wheel grip: the tide ends it within about 25 seconds. With T1, a hop frees it sooner.
  • Countdown: brakes are locked, so nobody rolls into a hit before GO. In 120 simulated starts on this build no buggy began in contact or tipped.
  • Seat fairness: the buggies swap sides every round. On the first, narrower build's Twin Humps arena the right-hand buggy won 40 of 40 mirrored bot rounds until sides alternated; after that seats split 20 to 20. I did not find the cause, so the real build needs a seat-balance test.
  • A plank falling on a helmet counts as own lid. The rule is “anything”, and it must stay that simple to read.
  • Opponent drops: the existing heartbeat pauses play, then offers WAIT or CLAIM WIN. No new work.
  • Host tab hidden: the sim pauses with the same reconnecting overlay rather than running blind.
  • One phone: each button captures its own touch, so a second thumb on the other pad never steals a press.
  • Reduced motion: no shake, zoom or slow-motion; the hit is marked by the banner and a flash only.
  • Colour is not the only cue: each buggy carries a pennant shape (triangle, circle, square, diamond) and a bar count on its door.

Main risk: the guest feels the host's delay, because the guest's own buggy is simulated on the other phone. Arcade driving is more sensitive to that than a puck is. It needs a test on two real networks before the look is polished, and a decision between snapshots (simple, laggy for the guest) and lockstep on the deterministic engine (fair, but both players feel a small delay).

Twists

Five ways to make it more than a clone. Four are switches on the prototype; the fifth is a mode on its start sheet.

T1 · Hop Ship first

Press both buttons and the buggy fires a short jet along its own “up”. It recharges in 2.2 seconds, shown on the pad.

Why
The source has no way to get above a rival except terrain. A hop turns every moment into a decision: leap now, bait theirs, or save it to climb out of the tide. Upside down, your “up” points at the ground, so it is no free rescue.
Risk
Chaining hops to stay airborne. The recharge is the control; lengthen it if rounds turn into hovering.
Cost
Small. One input bit, one impulse, one meter.

T2 · Spare lid Ship first

A player 2 or more points behind starts the round wearing a yellow hard hat. The first hit knocks the hat off instead of ending the round.

Why
A 4-0 scoreline in a first-to-5 is a walkover. One free mistake keeps the trailing player in it without touching the leader's score.
Risk
Can feel like charity. It is visible, it is one hit, and it never applies at 1 behind.
Cost
Small.

T3 · Loser picks Ship first

Whoever lost the round chooses the next arena from two cards.

Why
It gives the losing player something to do other than lose again, and it makes the arena list part of the mind game. The source just shuffles.
Risk
About 2 seconds added between rounds. Online it needs an auto-pick after 5 seconds.
Cost
Small. One room key and one short phase.

T4 · Wild rounds Second

Some rounds draw a rule card: Moon gravity, Black ice, Big heads or Turbo.

Why
It answers the one complaint found about the source, that the tracks run out. Six arenas times five conditions is thirty rounds that feel different.
Risk
Ice and Moon can feel out of control. Offer it as a host switch, off by default.
Cost
Small to medium. Each card is one multiplier, but each needs its own tuning pass.

T5 · Four-buggy melee Later

Up to four buggies in one arena. A bonked buggy stays as a wreck; the last helmet standing takes the point.

Why
The rest of this request is for games that seat four. This is the version of the duel that fits a party room and the night scoreboard.
Risk
High. It needs real-time transport for more than two peers, which Game Night does not have, and it is chaotic to read on a phone.
Cost
Large, on top of the base game.

Also considered, not prototyped: a co-op mode where the two players are roped together against two bot buggies (medium to large), and a “bonk cam” that replays the winning hit as a share card (medium).

Recommendation: v1 is the two-player duel with T1, T2 and T3 on. T4 follows as a host switch once the base numbers are settled. T5 waits for a decision on multi-peer real-time play, which other four-player games from this request may also need.
