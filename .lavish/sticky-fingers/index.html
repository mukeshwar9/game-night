Game Night design study · one of eight · source mechanic: “Money Grabber”

STICKY FINGERS

Loot drops on the table. Everyone reaches out of their own safe with a stretchy arm, drags it home and stashes it. Two hands on one bill rips it in half; two hands on one coin and the longer arm loses its grip.

This is Game Night's own take on the drag-money-to-your-safe mini-game: the same finger-wrestling on one screen, plus a grip rule that makes it fair and a reason to look before you grab. Play it below against bots or with a friend on a touch screen.

Play it

The table is live: the bots are playing each other until you press PLAY. With a mouse you have one hand. On a touch screen each finger is a hand.

    How to play

    1. Press on the table and your arm shoots out of your safe to your finger. Touch loot to grab it.
    2. Drag it into your safe, or let go mid-swing to flick it home. A flick can be intercepted.
    3. Fight for it. Two hands on a bill: pull apart and it rips, 1 point each instead of 3. Two hands on a coin or gem: the more stretched arm slips after a moment.
    4. Read before you grab. A pack with a blinking light is a dye pack: minus 3 and your hands are useless for a moment. Flick it into a rival's safe instead.

    What makes it fun

    • It is physical: fingers collide on the glass, and you can block a path with your hand.
    • Tearing a bill out of spite is funnier than winning it.
    • Zero rules to learn. The instruction is one sentence.

    What is weak

    • No reason to look: every item is good, so the best play is to mash.
    • Unlimited fingers favours big hands, and fingers hide the loot.
    • Reviews of the app say hard bots get “an unfair advantage” rather than playing better, and that games come with no explanation.
    • One device only. There is no way to play it with someone who is not in the room.

    Game Night's version: the rules

    Six rules, each one visible on the table. The first two are the source's idea; the rest are what make it Game Night's.

    1 · Reach and grab

    Press the table: your arm shoots to your finger and follows it exactly. An empty hand grabs the first loot it touches.

    2 · Stash or flick

    Loot scores the moment it enters a safe. Let go mid-swing and it keeps sliding: a flick home is faster, but anyone can catch it.

    3 · Paper rips

    Two hands on a bill stretch it. Pull apart and it tears: each side carries home a half worth 1. Ripping is how you deny the leader.

    4 · Metal slips

    Coins and gems cannot tear. The arm stretched further from its own safe has the weaker grip, and it thins as it stretches so you can see it. After a third of a second the weaker hand slips.

    5 · Three kinds of loot

    Coin 1, bill 3, gem 5. A gem is heavy: it drags behind your finger, so it stays stealable for longer.

    6 · One timed heist

    Sixty seconds on a clock in the middle of the table that every seat can read. Loot arrives faster as it runs down. A tie is settled by the next coin.

    Why a clock instead of the source's race to a target: everyone finishes together, the length is known (one minute), and a late swing is still possible. All the numbers here are starting values to tune in playtests, not settled facts.

    Seats, hands and where it runs

    Two players

    Safes at the two short ends, two hands each. This is the duel, and the only seating that also runs online in v1.

    Three players

    One safe at the bottom, two in the top corners. One hand each.

    Four players

    A safe in each corner, one hand each, so fingers come in diagonally and hide less of the middle.

    Why “two hands, or one”, not “all your fingers”

    The source lets you use every finger. Capping it keeps the table readable and stops the biggest hand winning. It also fits the hardware: iPhones are widely reported to track five touches at once and to cancel all of them on a sixth, so four players can only have one each. That limit should be confirmed on a real iPhone before the build.

    Twists

    Five ways to make it more than a clone. Four are playable in the table above: each button switches that twist on by itself and starts a round.

    Dye Packs SHIP FIRST

    One drop in ten is a banded pack with a blinking light. Stash it and you lose 3 and your hands are useless for a moment and a half. Flick it into a rival's safe and it is theirs.

    Why
    Gives a reason to look before grabbing, and turns flicking into an attack.
    Risk
    In a four-player game everyone may dump on the leader. The 3-point cost is small on purpose.
    Cost
    small

    Last Call SHIP FIRST

    For the final 10 seconds the table sprays loot and everything is worth double. The clock turns gold.

    Why
    A comeback is always possible, and the round ends on its loudest moment.
    Risk
    The first 50 seconds can feel like they matter less. If playtests say so, shorten it or drop the multiplier to 1.5.
    Cost
    small

    Arm Slap

    Tap a rival's stretched arm while it is carrying and it drops the loot and is stunned briefly. One slap per second.

    Why
    Long reaches become a real risk, which rewards playing your own half.
    Risk
    More chaos, and accidental slaps when fingers cross on one phone. Needs a clear miss-tap rule.
    Cost
    small

    Spotlight

    A guard's light patrols the table. Carry loot through it for a third of a second and you are caught: you drop it and freeze. Empty hands are safe.

    Why
    Adds timing and routes to a game that is otherwise pure speed.
    Risk
    One more thing on a small screen with four players. Better as an optional mode than the default.
    Cost
    small to medium

    Clean Sweep (co-op)

    Everyone against the clock. Loot is colour-tagged and only scores in the matching safe, so you pass it across the table by flicking. Three wrong stashes trip the alarm.

    Why
    The same table becomes a team game, which Game Night already does with Updraft Co-op and Pulp Harvest.
    Risk
    A second ruleset to teach and balance. Not in the prototype.
    Cost
    medium

    What the build needs

    • A pure sim module with tests: loot, hands, grip, tear, slip, flick, scoring, and the bot.
    • A table renderer on canvas, drawn from theme tokens. It needs four new art tokens (two felt shades, rim, metal) per theme, or a mix of existing ones.
    • A one-phone page for 2 to 4 and a solo page with bot levels, following the Puck Rush pattern.
    • An online duel page on the existing host and guest hooks, with a small snapshot format for hands and loot.
    • Registry entry, icon, HOW TO PLAY text, sounds and a two-client test. No database rule changes for the duel.
