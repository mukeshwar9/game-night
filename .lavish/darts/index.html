Steady Hand

Darts for two to four, on one phone or in a room. Your hand shakes in a slow rhythm; you pick a segment, wait for the shake to tighten, and let go. First to bring 301 down to exactly zero wins.

The game on this page is real. Pick how you throw on its setup screen, press START, then hold on the board, drag to move the aim, and release when the ring is small. It starts at 101 so a leg takes about a minute. The THEME menu under the phone restyles the whole game, board included.

  • SETUP
    Choose your throw before the leg. Steady aim or one button, picked once for the whole room so every seat plays the same game.
  • THROW
    Hold, drag, let go. The crosshair appears near your finger but never exactly under it, a little off in a random direction, so every dart starts with a correction. From there it moves like a trackpad. The ring around the crosshair is your shake: it breathes in and out every second and a half.
  • SCORE
    Three darts a visit, counted down from 301. Outer ring doubles, inner ring trebles, bull is 50. Go below zero and the visit is wiped.
  • FINISH
    Land on exactly zero. When one dart can finish, every segment that does it glows gold.
  • SEATS
    2 to 4, same rules everywhere: pass the phone, sit face to face, or each on your own phone in a Game Night room. A bot fills in for solo.

Recommendation: build it, medium effort. It can sit on Archery's room model almost unchanged, so the new work is the scoring logic, the stage and the throw feel.

Agreed so far: Turf and Nerves are in, the throw style is picked on the setup screen, and the board follows the player's theme. Still open: the name, player counts and leg length, at the bottom of this page.

What the source game does

The app is a one-device party collection (100M+ installs, 4.3 stars). Its darts is a pure timing game: a marker slides along a horizontal meter, a second along a vertical one, and one tap on your corner button stops each. You never choose where to aim; you only choose when.

It is a race from 301 to exactly zero, the view turns to face whoever is throwing, and the camera pushes in on every hit. That part works. What it lacks is any decision: no safe target, no reason to watch the other player, and near the end a single unlucky number can drag on.

So the design keeps the countdown, the timing and the hit moment, and adds the two things it is missing: a choice of target, and a reason to care about the other players' darts.

How ours plays

A leg, start to finish

Everyone starts on 301. A visit is three darts. The top of the screen shows each player's remaining score, three pips for darts in hand, and the three chips under the board fill with what you just threw.

Bust: drop below zero and the visit is wiped back to where it started. Finish: any segment that lands on exactly zero, with double-out as an optional house rule for players who know darts.

Fair seats: throwing first is an edge, so the round always completes. If two players check out in the same round, fewer darts wins, then a one-dart nearest-the-bull shoot-off, which Archery already has.

Match: two players play best of three legs with the first throw alternating; three or four play a single leg. At 301 a two-player leg should run about three minutes.

Who sits where

Five twists

Each one answers a weakness of plain darts. Nerves and Turf are confirmed for the first build; the TRY IT buttons start a leg with that twist on.

Nerves · confirmed

Whoever is leading shakes up to 25% more; whoever is last shakes 15% less. The seat card says SHAKY, EVEN or CALM, so nobody is surprised.

WHY IT IS MORE FUN
Legs stay close without touching anyone's score, and it reads as a story: the leader choking on the finish.
RISK
A strong player may feel taxed. It is one visible switch in the lobby.
BUILD COST
Small. One multiplier in the logic module.

Turf · confirmed

A second mode with no arithmetic. Hit a wedge to paint it your colour and shape. A double locks it for good; a treble takes both neighbours too; the bull is two bonus points. Most wedges after five rounds wins.

WHY IT IS MORE FUN
Every dart can take something from someone, which is what three and four players need. Children can play it; nobody subtracts.
RISK
Two players may gang up on the leader. Locks are the counter.
BUILD COST
Small to medium. A second scoring function on the same throw and board.

Knockback

Your last visit's darts stay in the board as ghosts until you throw again. A rival who lands in the same segment pushes your score back up by its value, and still scores it themselves.

WHY IT IS MORE FUN
Plain darts is two people playing alone. This makes a big treble a liability and gives a reason to watch the other turn.
RISK
Legs can run long. Capped: no score goes above its start, and ghosts last one round.
BUILD COST
Small. The landing points are already stored.

Balloons

A balloon drifts across the board each visit. Pop it and your next dart has almost no shake. The dart still scores wherever it lands, so chasing the balloon costs you position.

WHY IT IS MORE FUN
A moving target on a still board, and a small gamble every visit. Room for more perks later: a shield against one bust, a doubled dart.
RISK
Clutter on a board that needs to stay readable. One balloon at a time.
BUILD COST
Medium. The balloon path must be seeded so every phone sees the same one.

Daily checkout

A solo puzzle on the existing daily page: everyone gets the same number, say 127, and the fewest darts to finish it wins the day.

Shown, not built: it needs the daily board, not the room.

WHY IT IS MORE FUN
A reason to open the game alone, and it teaches finishing, which makes the multiplayer game better.
RISK
Scores are reported by the player's own phone, as with the other daily games.
BUILD COST
Small to medium, after the main game exists.
