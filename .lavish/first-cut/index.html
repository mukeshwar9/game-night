THE GAME I STUDIED

HOW THE ORIGINAL PLAYS

  1. One icon sits in the middle and swaps to a new one roughly every second.
  2. Three of the icons are fruit (the app's own help screen lists them). The rest are round lookalikes: a flower, a wrapped gift, a striped ball. They are drawn to resemble the fruit at a glance.
  3. Each player has one button. Tap it and your blade swings at whatever is showing.
  4. Fruit: the first tap scores 1 and the fruit splits. The slower player gets nothing.
  5. Lookalike: your blade sticks in it and your button goes dark. In the clip I stepped through, a stuck player stayed locked for more than a second after a real fruit had already appeared, so the other player had a free cut.
  6. First to 10 wins. A fast two-player game is about 22 seconds; an ordinary one about a minute.

WHY IT WORKS

You learn it in three seconds. The lookalikes make you fight your own reflex, which is funnier than a plain speed test. On one phone you can see the other person's thumb twitch, so people start faking each other out without any rule for it.

WHERE IT IS THIN

Only three fruit and three lookalikes, so after two games you know them all and it becomes pure speed. The slower player can lose 10–0 with no way back. Nothing changes from game to game. The twists below go at exactly these three.

GAME NIGHT'S VERSION

THE RULES, IN FOUR PICTURES

1 · A FRUIT SHOWS

Anything on the plate stays for about 1.2 to 1.9 seconds.

2 · FIRST TAP CUTS IT

One point to the fastest thumb. Everyone else gets nothing.

3 · A LOOKALIKE SHOWS

Each fruit has a twin that is not a fruit. Leave it.

4 · TAP IT, YOU ARE BLOCKED

Your katana stops dead at its edge and does not cut. It lifts back slowly through the next item, so you miss that one.

These are my own drawings for the board, not the source app's art. Twins differ by shape and pattern (seams, panels, a cap) as well as colour, so the game still works for colour-blind players. A build would turn them into sprite sheets in this style, and can add more pairs later (kiwi and a ball of wool, cherry and a bomb).

WHERE EVERYONE SITS ON ONE PHONE

Buttons are big slabs, not small corner dots: a whole end of the phone for two players, half an end each for four. The plate and every item are round and have no "up", so nobody reads them upside down. Scores face their owner.

HOW IT STAYS FAIR ONLINE

Online there is no shared screen, so "who tapped first" cannot mean "whose message arrived first": that would hand the game to the best wifi.

Instead every phone plays the same pre-agreed sequence of items (one seed for the room, exactly how Reaction Time and Pulp Rush already work) and times its own player from the moment the fruit appeared on that phone. The fruit goes to the shortest time. You see the result about a third of a second after the cut.

Trade-off: times are reported by each phone, so a cheat could send a fake fast time. Reaction Time has the same weakness today. Fine for friends; I would keep this game off the verified leaderboard.

MORE THAN A CLONE

FIVE TWISTS

SHIP FIRST COST: SMALL

A · RULE FLIP

What changes: a card by the plate says what counts right now: ANY FRUIT, CITRUS ONLY, NO CITRUS, RED OR GREEN. It flips every three cuts. An apple is now a trap during CITRUS ONLY.

Why it is more fun: it stops the game becoming pure speed. You have to read, and the habit you just built is the thing that gets your katana blocked.

Risk: too much to read at four players. Kept to four short rules and a one-second pause when it flips.

SHIP FIRST COST: SMALL

B · GOLD & ROTTEN, WITH A HAND UP

What changes: now and then a fruit is gold: worth 3 but gone in half a second. Now and then a real fruit is rotten (spots and a fly): swing at it and you lose a point and your katana is blocked. And whoever is 3 or more behind the leader gets a quicker katana: after a wrong cut it is free again in time for the next item.

Why it is more fun: a 7–2 game can turn on one gold fruit, so nobody stops trying. Rotten fruit punishes the player who has stopped looking and is just tapping on colour.

Risk: gold feels random if it is too common. Set at about one in eight fruit.

SECOND WAVE COST: MEDIUM

C · ORDER UP (CO-OP)

What changes: everyone is one kitchen. Each fruit arrives with a ring in one player's colour and only that player may cut it. A wrong hand, a lookalike, or a fruit nobody cut costs one of three shared hearts. It speeds up. The score is how many the team served.

Why it is more fun: the same reflex, but now you are shouting "yours!" instead of beating each other. Game Night already groups co-op games together, and this gives a couple or a parent and child something to do on the same side.

Risk: needs its own balance pass and its own end screen.

SECOND WAVE COST: MEDIUM

D · THE FEINT (ONLINE)

What changes: across a table you can fake a twitch with your thumb. Online you cannot, so each player gets two feints a round: a second small button that makes your blade flinch on everyone else's screen without committing.

Why it is more fun: it brings the best part of the same-table game, the mind games, into online rooms.

Risk: a second button dilutes "one button each". Needs playtesting before it earns its place. Not in the prototype.

SECOND WAVE COST: SMALL–MEDIUM

E · THE DEALER (3–4 PLAYERS)

What changes: one player is the dealer for a round. They do not cut. Their button puts the next item on the plate when they choose, so they control the rhythm: three quick ones, a long pause, then a lookalike. The dealer scores a point for every wrong cut they cause. The job passes round.

Why it is more fun: it gives the third and fourth players a different job, and the dealer is playing the people, not the plate.

Risk: the dealer cannot choose what comes next (that would be unfair), only when, and that has to feel like enough. Not in the prototype.

ALSO IN THE PROTOTYPE

THE RECEIPT

The end screen shows each player's average cut time in milliseconds and how many times they were blocked. It is the bit people will screenshot ("312 ms, never blocked"), it fits the night scoreboard and SHARE RECAP the app already has, and it costs nothing: the game has to measure those times anyway.

ONE FOR LATER

The app's README plans a "who goes first?" step for other games. A single sudden-death fruit from this game would be a fun way to settle it. Worth remembering when that feature is built; not part of this one.

WHAT THE BUILD NEEDS

NEW CODE

One rules module with tests (the item sequence from a seed, who wins each fruit, the stuck katana, the twists, the bot). One arena component. One page for online rooms, one for one-phone play, one for solo against the bot. An icon, the HOW TO PLAY text, a registry entry.

SERVER SIDE

Nothing new. It uses the existing race rooms (the same ones Reaction Time and Pulp Rush use), with each player's cut times stored as their race stats. One small addition to the database rules and a test for it. No peer-to-peer connection needed.

ART AND SOUND

Eight shaded sprites (four fruit, four twins), four cut faces and a katana, in the style shown in the prototype. The table, board, buttons and effects need no art: they are drawn from the app's theme colours, so every existing theme works with no extra work, and so will any theme added later. That is a different look from the app's pixel art, so this game would be the first to carry it. Sounds: a swing, a chop, a clang, the win sting. The background music follows the "reflex" category automatically.
