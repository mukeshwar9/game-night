GAME NIGHT · NEW GAME DESIGN · WORKING NAME

SIDE KICK

A one-minute bike sprint for up to four riders. Steer through traffic, pull alongside a rival and boot them sideways: into a van, onto the grass, or clean off the bike. First over the line wins the race; three races make a cup.

The classic it studies is the 1991 to 1995 Road Rash line. Nothing is copied from it: the name, riders, road, art and sounds here are Game Night's own, and the tone is slapstick (helmets, stars, no blood).

MEADOW RUN

You start last. Catch the pack, kick your way through, take the flag.

WHAT TO TRY

  1. Catch the pack. The throttle is automatic. Hold a steer button to change lanes; the road pushes you outward in corners.
  2. Sit behind a rider for half a second: SLIPSTREAM lights up and you close the gap 10% faster.
  3. Pull alongside and kick toward them. A hit shoves them a lane sideways, costs them speed and one balance pip.
  4. Kick someone into traffic or off the tarmac. A car is an instant wipeout; grass caps speed at 42%.
  5. Take three kicks while behind: you are down 1.2 s, come back at 55% speed, and CATCH-UP runs until you have won back the gap you lost. Fall while leading and it is the full 1.9 s with no boost.
  6. Kick three or four times in a row. Every kick rocks your own bike and fills the strain bar on the kick buttons. They turn red when you are close; one more and you throw yourself off. A whiff costs more than a hit.
  7. Check your mirrors. The two small mirrors show who is behind you and on which side. A rim lights up in a rider's colour when they are close enough to be lining up a kick.
  8. Hold BOOST on a straight: +18% speed while the tank lasts (2 s from full), then it refills in 8 s. You start with half a tank.

On a keyboard: ← → steer, A kick left, D kick right, SPACE boost. On a phone, left thumb steers, right thumb kicks, either thumb reaches BOOST in the middle.

What is real here: the road, steering, slipstream, kicks, balance, knock-off and remount, traffic, oil, three bots, places and the result sheet. What is stand-in: bot brains (random lane and kick choices with a rubber band), every number (all are starting values to tune by playtest), and the synth sounds. Before START the four bots race each other so the road is never still.

ROUND 5 · YOUR TWO RULES

KICKS COST BALANCE · BOOST RECHARGES

KICK STRAIN

Each kick rocks your own bike harder the fuller the bar is, pushes you slightly away from the kick and dulls your steering by up to 40%. A hit adds 0.30, a miss 0.40; it holds for 0.4 s then drains at 0.25 a second. So a kick is always available, but a flurry is a gamble and mashing is self-destruction. Bots stop kicking above 0.55.

BOOST

Hold to burn, let go to keep what is left. +18% top speed and double pickup while it burns. The tank empties in 2 s and refills in 8, after a half-second pause. It needs 15% to light. It cuts out if you are staggered and is lost for that moment if you fall, so a boosting rider is worth kicking. With a slipstream it stacks to about +30%: the overtaking move.

Why these fit together: boost gets you level with a rival, strain decides how many kicks you can afford once you are there, and catch-up keeps whoever loses that exchange in the race. All numbers are first guesses for playtest.

ROUND 3 · REALISTIC AND ANIMATED

ONE SUN, REAL WEIGHT

The flat shapes are gone. The scene now has a single light, up and to the right, and everything answers to it: lit and shaded sides, soft shadows on the ground, haze with distance, glare on the tarmac. Motion comes from springs and world-space effects instead of on/off states. It is still one 2D canvas and still follows the theme; it held 60 frames a second in the test browser. Press START above and watch for these.

MATERIALS AND LIGHT

  • Rubber, brushed metal, paint and glass each shade differently; helmets are lit spheres.
  • Cars: painted bodies with a sky reflection in the rear glass, lamp clusters, chrome bumper, number plate.
  • Trees and bushes built from lit leaf clumps; cottages with shingles, window reflections and eave shadows.
  • Sky with a blooming sun, shaded clouds in two depths, two-face mountains with snow, haze toward the horizon.
  • Night themes add real light: headlight beams, glowing tail lamps, street-light pools, blinking tower beacons.

ANIMATION

  • Kick: knee tucks in, leg snaps out past full reach and settles, then recovers. The helmet turns toward the target.
  • A hit rocks the victim on a spring and squashes them for a beat; the kicker recoils the other way.
  • Suspension loads over crests and dips. Engine buzz, body bob, jacket hem flutter, rolling tread.
  • A fall: the bike slides on its side throwing sparks, the rider tumbles with loose limbs, sits dazed, then stands the bike back up.
  • Brake lamp brightens when a rider slows.

WORLD AND CAMERA

  • Exhaust smoke, dust, grass chips, sparks and skid marks live on the road, so you ride through what the rider ahead left behind.
  • Trees sway, flags and bunting ripple, grass moves, clouds drift, birds cross, street lights flicker.
  • Camera trails the bike, rolls into corners, pulls back in a slipstream, punches in on a landed kick and out when you take one.
  • Reduced-motion setting turns the camera roll and shake off.

How it stays fast: static objects are painted once per theme into small images and reused; only the four riders, cloth and effects are drawn fresh each frame. Not yet measured on a real low-end phone.

ROUND 2 · YOUR THEMES (NOW WITH THE ROUND 3 ART)

ONE ROAD, EVERY THEME

The race now takes its colours from the app's theme tokens. Nothing in the scene has its own colour any more: sky, grass, road, kerbs, riders, cars, trees and the HUD are each a token or a mix of two, so every theme gets a matching road and a new theme needs no new art. Light themes get a sun, clouds and snow peaks; dark themes get stars, a striped sun, glowing tail lamps, lit towers and street lights. These six are the same live race, all running now.

HOW A THEME BECOMES A ROAD

  • Seat colours are the theme's four player colours.
  • Light themes name one token for sky and one for ground; everything else is derived.
  • Dark themes need no choices: sky from the background, glow from player two, kerbs from player one, centre line from the action colour.
  • Light and shadow tints are also mixed from the theme, so shading never leaves the palette.

SMOOTH OR PIXEL

The STYLE switch above the phone redraws the same scene at half resolution with hard pixels, closer to the app's pixel font and sprites. SMOOTH reads better at speed; PIXEL is more on-brand. Decided: both ship, as a player setting next to the theme picker.

HOW IT PLAYS

WHERE A KICK LANDS

The kick is a short side zone, checked on one frame 90 ms after the press. You must be level with the target: no kicking forward at a rider you are chasing, which is what keeps slipstream and kicking as two separate skills.

HOW IT PLAYS

BALANCE, NOT HEALTH

Nobody is eliminated, and a fall while you are behind is short: the catch-up boost lasts only until your gap to the rider ahead is back to what it was, so it repairs the fall and never pays for one. The classic's stamina bar, bike-damage bar, wrecks, fines and running back to the bike collapse into one rule a phone player reads at a glance: three pips, a short fall, an automatic remount.

ONE PHONE, MANY RIDERS?

Not for v1. A chase view needs the whole screen per rider: two riders at opposite ends of one phone get a 360 by 300 strip each with their thumbs covering a third of it, and four is unreadable. Puck Rush and Animal Stack work on one phone because everyone shares one table; a chase camera has no shared table. A shared top-down view would work on one phone but is a different, weaker game (see the view comparison).

SOLO

The prototype is the solo mode: you against three bots, EASY / NORMAL / HARD changing bot top speed, kick rate and how well they dodge traffic. Same page pattern as the other /demo pages, no network.

DECISION 1 · VIEW

THREE WAYS TO LOOK AT THE ROAD

A · CHASE CAM (PSEUDO-3D) RECOMMENDED

The classic feel. Speed sells itself, a rival beside you is big and level so the kick reads, traffic arrives as a threat from the horizon. One canvas, ~150 road slices and ~60 sprites per frame: comfortable on phones.

B · TOP-DOWN

Cheapest to draw and to sync, all four riders always visible, could share one phone. But bikes are 12 px slivers, a kick is a twitch, and there is no sense of speed. It would read as a second Tron or Paint Turf.

C · SIDE / THREE-QUARTER

Bikes look great in profile, but lanes stack into depth: "is that rider beside me or one lane over" is the exact thing a kick needs and the one thing this view hides. Oncoming traffic gets about a third of a second of warning.

THE LOOK, PIECE BY PIECE

RIDER AND BIKE

Drawn in code, seen from behind and lit from the right: fat rear tyre with rolling tread, tail in the darker seat colour with a red lamp, twin pipes, jacket in the seat colour with shoulder pads and a race number, crested helmet with a colour band. The bike leans up to 17° into a steer. A kick throws the leg out with a yellow-soled boot and a white arc; the torso shifts the other way. Build adds the player's avatar face on the helmet back.

ROAD AND WORLD

Four lanes, striped kerbs, painted edge and centre lines. Ground in two alternating bands so speed reads even on a straight. Pines, round trees, bushes, flowers, street lights, billboards, cottages or towers, chevron boards before hard corners, bunting poles in the four seat colours, a START arch and a chequered FINISH arch. Sky, sun, clouds, mountains and two hill layers slide at different rates as the road bends. Distance fades into haze. Every colour comes from the active theme.

SPEED AND CONTACT

Above 86% speed streaks fly out from the vanishing point and the vignette tightens; in a slipstream they turn warm and the camera pulls back. A landed kick: 70 ms freeze, a flash ring off the victim, a POW burst, a camera punch-in, the victim rocking on a spring. Taking one: bigger shake, punch-out and a red banner. A fall: sparks and skid marks, a tumbling rider, three stars, then the bike is stood back up and the remount blinks.

HUD

Glass chips only at the corners: place (large), time and a four-dot progress rail, speed, three balance pips that turn gold when you are hurt, and a chip for SLIPSTREAM, BOOST or CATCH-UP that springs in. Under the top chips sit two small rear mirrors showing who is behind and on which side; a rim glows in a rider's colour when they are about to reach you. One line of banner text at a time, 1.3 s. Controls sit under the road, never over it.

SOUND

Engine hum that rises with speed and wobbles in a stagger; whoosh on a swing; thud plus square-wave drop on a hit; long noise burst on a fall; rising two-note sting when you unseat someone; 3-2-1 beeps; a four-note finish. In the build these go through sounds.js and the scene uses useMusicScene('game').

HAPTICS

On contact, never after the network: 25 ms when your kick lands, a double buzz when you are hit, a long triple on a fall, a short roll at the flag. Through haptics.js so the existing setting and caps apply. Reduced motion drops the shake, streaks and tumble and keeps the banners.

ONLINE · THE HARD PART

HOW A KICK CROSSES THE NETWORK

Each phone simulates only its own bike on the same seeded road, the way Updraft already races. Rivals are ghosts moved forward from their last reported position and speed. Traffic and oil are a pure function of the seed and the shared race clock, so every phone sees the same van in the same lane without sending it.

A kick is decided on the kicker's phone ("what you see is what you hit") and sent as an event; the victim's phone applies the shove, pip and stagger when it arrives. The victim feels a hit 100 to 300 ms after the leg went out, which is about the length of the kick animation itself.

Why not the existing peer-to-peer link: realtime/rtc.js connects exactly two seats (X hosts, O joins). Four riders would need six links or a host relay, new signalling, and a host whose phone decides everyone's race. The relay through the room needs no new transport.

LATENCY RISKS

  • "I was not next to them": the victim saw the kicker a bike length away. Softened by the visible wind-up, the 1.25-length reach and the kicker's ghost snapping level when the event lands.
  • Two riders kick each other at once: both land. Reads as a trade, costs both a pip.
  • Shield wins ties: a kick that arrives during the 1.5 s remount shield is dropped and the kicker sees CLANG.
  • Position writes: 4 riders at 10 Hz is ~2,600 small writes a race. Needs a cost and rate check on the emulator before the build commits to it.

ROUND 9 · THE RIDER IS YOUR AVATAR, FROM BEHIND, NO HELMET

Done as you asked. With RIDERS set to AVATARS, the rider on the bike is the player's avatar seen from the back: their hair or hat, their skin tone at the neck, their own top colour for the jacket and sleeves. The helmet is gone. The seat colour moves to a race bib with the rider's number on their back, and stays on the bike tail, the name tag and the kerb bunting, so you can still tell seats apart at a glance.

How the back is made. The avatar kit only draws fronts, so the back is built by rule from the kit's own parts: same head shape and body, face left off, the back of the head filled with the hair colour, the fringe keeping its outline, long hair falling over the shoulders, hats kept, the picture mirrored. Nobody has to draw a second version of any cosmetic. Bald avatars show skin; a hat that hides the hair hides it from behind too.

ROUND 7 · WHAT NEXT

YES TO TRACKS, AND THEY ARE CHEAP

A track here is a short list of road pieces plus two dials: how busy and how wooded. So I added three more to the prototype to show it; pick one with the TRACK buttons above the phone. The same builder can also make a fresh road from the race seed, so online every race could be a road nobody has seen, identical on all four phones.

MEADOW RUN

The all-rounder you have been playing. Medium bends, rolling hills, light traffic.

SWITCHBACK PASS

Hard corners back to back over big crests, thick forest, few cars. Steering and slipstream decide it; being kicked wide on a bend costs most.

RUSH HOUR

Long straights through town, a car every second or so, more oil. Boost and nerve decide it; kicks send people into bumpers.

RANDOM ROAD

A new road each press, built from a seed. Proof that tracks can be endless without anyone drawing them.
